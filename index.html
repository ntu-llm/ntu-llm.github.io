Course outline

  1. Introduction to Large Language Models

    • Overview of foundation models and the generative AI landscape
    • Evolution of LLMs from pretrained transformers to instruction-tuned and reasoning models
    • Typical applications, limitations, and emerging capabilities of LLMs
    • The role of LLMs in modern AI ecosystems and their societal impact

  2. Architecture and Training of LLMs

    • Transformer architecture: tokenization, embeddings, self-attention, and positional encoding
    • Pretraining objectives and large-scale data strategies
    • Fine-tuning, instruction-tuning, and parameter-efficient adaptation (LoRA, QLoRA)
    • Scaling laws, computational efficiency, and optimization trade-offs
    • Comparison of closed-source and open-source model architectures

  3. LLM Inference and Evaluation

    • Inference optimization and decoding methods (temperature, top-k/top-p sampling)
    • Quantization, caching, and memory management for efficient deployment
    • Evaluation frameworks: intrinsic metrics (perplexity, BLEU, ROUGE) and extrinsic measures (accuracy, factuality, robustness)
    • Analysis and mitigation of hallucination, bias, and fairness issues
    • Practical strategies for prompt testing and performance benchmarking

  4. Principles of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

    • Motivation for retrieval augmentation and its role in improving factual grounding
    • RAG architecture and workflow: document chunking, embedding, indexing, and retrieval
    • Vector databases and similarity search (FAISS, Pinecone, Chroma, Weaviate)
    • Context retrieval, query–document alignment, and response generation integration
    • Strengths and constraints of RAG systems compared with standalone LLMs

  5. Advanced RAG Techniques

    • Hybrid retrieval (dense–sparse) and reranking methods
    • Multi-document and multimodal RAG pipelines
    • Performance tuning: batching, caching, and contextual compression
    • Evaluation of RAG systems: grounding quality, coherence, latency, and efficiency

  6. Responsible and Applied RAG Deployment

    • Governance, transparency, and safety guardrails for generative AI systems
    • Responsible AI and sustainability considerations in deployment
    • Case studies of LLM + RAG applications in education, healthcare, and enterprise settings
    • Best practices for secure integration, monitoring, and continuous improvement
